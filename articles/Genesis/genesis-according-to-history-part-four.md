<h2>Introduction</h2>

<p>The following article continues to draw on Gleason L. Archer Jr., <em>A Survey of Old Testament Introduction</em> (Chicago: Moody Press), pp. 209-212. It shows how the fossil finds of early man and the genealogies of <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+5&version=ESV">Genesis 5</a> and <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+10&version=ESV">10</a> raise a hard question about the age of the human race, and it sets out the author’s proposal that these early races were prior to Adam. This is a proposal and not a settled doctrine, and Bible-believing Christians hold a range of views on it.</p>

<p>With that said, I proceed to the summary.</p>

<h2>Main Discussion</h2>

<p>Each part below builds on the one before it, so it is best read in order.</p>

<h3>1. The Problem of Early Man</h3>

<p>Since the 1850s, discoveries of prehistoric fossils and artifacts have raised a hard question for readers of Genesis: how old is the human race, and how does that fit the genealogies of Genesis 5 and 10?</p>

<p>Finds such as the Swanscombe man in England, Pithecanthropus in Java and Sinanthropus in China have been dated by modern estimates to between 200,000 and 500,000 years ago. The Neanderthals are dated from about 50,000 to 100,000 years ago, and the Zinjanthropus of Tanganyika has been given an age of about 1,750,000 years by the potassium-argon method.</p>

<h4>More than apes</h4>

<p>These beings differ markedly from <em>Homo sapiens</em>, and some anthropologists regard the differences as comparable to those between a gorilla and a chimpanzee. Yet they cannot be dismissed as simple apes.</p>

<p>Their remains are found with stone tools and traces of cooking fires. At Neanderthal sites there is apparently burial with tools, as if they believed in life after death, and some crude statuettes and perhaps some cave paintings may belong to them.</p>

<p>It is possible that later research will show the dating methods to be faulty. But it is unlikely that the dates can be brought within the span suggested by the genealogies, unless those lists are not meant as time indicators, or unless these creatures were not descended from Adam.</p>

<p>So there are two places to look for an answer: in how the genealogies work, and in who these early creatures were.</p>

<h3>2. How Much Time Do the Genealogies Allow?</h3>

<p>Some say the Bible does not tell us how long ago man was created. The author thinks this an overstatement.</p>

<p>To place Adam at 200,000 B.C. would require a hundred times more generations missing than present. In Matthew 1 only about seven links are missing out of forty-two over 2,000 years, roughly one in six. That is slender ground for claiming that nearly two thousand generations were dropped between Adam and Abraham.</p>

<p>The <em>Westminster Dictionary of the Bible</em> lists three readings of Genesis 5 and 10.</p>

<h4>1. Strict, unbroken generations</h4>

<p>This gives about 1,656 years from Adam to the Flood and about 290 from the Flood to Abraham, a total of 1,946 years. But the text gives no such total, and the ten-and-ten grouping looks schematic, like the three groups of fourteen in Matthew 1 where links are demonstrably missing. Luke 3:36 also mentions a Cainan between Arphaxad and Shelah who is absent from <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+10%3A24&version=ESV">Genesis 10:24</a>. This reading leaves too little room for the attested history of Egypt.</p>

<h4>2. Selective genealogies</h4>

<p>Only prominent ancestors are named, and an unknown number are omitted. In Hebrew usage “A begat B” can mean that B was a descendant of A, since grandfathers are sometimes said to have begotten grandsons, as in <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Chronicles+7%3A13&version=ESV">1 Chronicles 7:13</a>. The patriarchs’ ages are real lifetimes. This allows perhaps five or six thousand years from Adam to Abraham.</p>

<p>Abraham may also not have been Terah’s eldest son (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+11%3A26-32&version=ESV">Genesis 11:26-32</a>; <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+12%3A4&version=ESV">12:4</a>), and Acts 7:4 says he left Haran after Terah’s death.</p>

<h4>3. Family leadership</h4>

<p>The names in <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+5&version=ESV">Genesis 5</a> stand for a man and his direct line, with the figures added almost end to end, giving over 8,000 years before the flood. A difficulty is that Seth is the oldest surviving son of Adam named, apart from Cain, so it is hard to see who else headed the line before him.</p>

<p>On balance the author considers the second reading the most tenable.</p>

<h3>3. Who Were the Early Races?</h3>

<p>If the genealogies cannot be stretched to 200,000 years, how are the fossil finds to be explained? The author proposes that these races, from Pithecanthropus and Neanderthal to perhaps even Cro-Magnon, were <strong>prior to Adam</strong> and not involved in the covenant made with him. Whether they had souls is left open.</p>

<p>His reasons rest on Scripture:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Genesis 1:26-27</strong> suggests that God was making a qualitatively different being when He created Adam, uniquely made in His image and given the breath of God and a spiritual nature (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1%3A26-27&version=ESV">Genesis 1:26-27</a>).</li>
  <li><strong>Romans 5:12-21</strong> requires that mankind from Adam onward be literally descended from him, since he entered into covenant with God as representative of the whole human race.</li>
</ul>

<p>On this view there was no true genetic link between Adam and the earlier races, however close their skeletons may be to ours. What matters is whether they had a truly human soul and personality.</p>

<p>They may have been destroyed by God for reasons unknown before Adam was created. Adam was the first man made in the spiritual image of God, and science has no evidence to disprove that.</p>

<h3>4. A Word of Caution</h3>

<p>This is a proposal, not a settled doctrine. Bible-believing Christians hold a range of views on these questions, from a young earth to the day-age approach and beyond, and the author himself says that some matters must be left open. We should hold such positions with humility, test them against Scripture, and keep at the center the truth that God made the heavens and the earth, made man in His own image, and began the work of redemption through a chosen family.</p>

<h3>5. Why This Matters</h3>

<p>What makes Adam special is not only when he lived but what he is. Romans 5 ties the whole human story, sin and redemption alike, to one man and to Christ. That is why the question of who Adam was cannot be treated as a side issue: the gospel’s picture of humanity rests on it.</p>

<h3>6. Key Takeaways</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Fossil finds dated at tens or hundreds of thousands of years raise a hard question for the genealogies of Genesis 5 and 10.</li>
  <li>Three readings of the genealogies exist, and the author finds the “selective genealogies” reading the most tenable.</li>
  <li>He proposes that the early races were prior to Adam, who was the first man made in the spiritual image of God.</li>
  <li>This remains a proposal, and Bible-believing Christians hold a range of views.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>Across the four parts of this series we have seen that:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Genesis comes from one author, most plausibly Moses, working under the Spirit from older family records (<a href="{{ gp1.url | relative_url }}">PT 1</a>).</li>
  <li>It stands apart from the pagan myths in teaching creation from nothing, and its “days” may be understood as stages of God’s work (<a href="{{ gp2.url | relative_url }}">PT 2</a>).</li>
  <li>The Darwinian account of origins has serious weaknesses in the author’s view and was historically used to remove God from the picture (<a href="{{ gp3.url | relative_url }}">PT 3</a>).</li>
  <li>The questions of early man and the genealogies call for careful reading of both Scripture and science (PT 4, this article).</li>
</ul>

<p>Genesis is not a textbook of science, but it is true history, and it tells us where we came from, why the world is broken, and how God began the long work of redemption.</p>
