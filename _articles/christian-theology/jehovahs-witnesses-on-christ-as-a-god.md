## Introduction

Jehovah’s Witnesses teach that Jesus is “a god” but not God Almighty. Before answering that teaching, we must state it accurately. An answer aimed at something Witnesses do not believe is easily dismissed, and it fails the basic duty of honesty. This article sets out what they teach about Christ, then answers each point from Scripture.

## What Witnesses teach about Jesus

- **Jehovah alone is God Almighty.** Jesus is a separate, subordinate being.
- **Jesus is God’s first creation.** They cite [Colossians 1:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+1%3A15&version=NKJV) (“firstborn of all creation”), [Revelation 3:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+3%3A14&version=NKJV) and [Proverbs 8:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+8%3A22&version=NKJV).
- **Before coming to earth, Jesus was Michael the archangel.**
- **At his baptism he became the Messiah and a “spiritual son.”** They hold that he was “born again” and anointed at that point.
- **He rose as a spirit, not in the body.** They teach that Jehovah disposed of the body and that Jesus appeared in temporary materialized bodies. Only after the resurrection, they say, was he given immortality and a divine nature.
- **The soul is not immortal.** Death is a state of non-existence until the resurrection.
- **The Trinity is rejected.** The holy spirit is God’s active force, not a person.

Two clarifications matter. Witnesses do call themselves Christians, so the honest charge is that their teaching departs from historic Christianity, not that they deny the name. And they have nothing to do with the Sabbath. The name “Jehovah’s Witnesses” comes from [Isaiah 43:10-12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+43%3A10-12&version=NKJV), not from Jewish practice. The best historical parallel to their Christology is **Arianism**, the fourth-century teaching, condemned at Nicaea in AD 325, that the Son is a created being.

## Was Jesus made the Son at his baptism?

At the Jordan the Father said, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” ([Matthew 3:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+3%3A17&version=NKJV)), and John the Baptist testified, “this is the Son of God” ([John 1:34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A34&version=NKJV)). Both statements **declare** a Sonship that already existed. They do not create it.

- **Before his birth**, the angel said that the holy one to be born “will be called the Son of God” ([Luke 1:35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+1%3A35&version=NKJV)).
- **The Son was sent.** “God sent forth His Son, born of a woman” ([Galatians 4:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians+4%3A4&version=NKJV)), and “God sent His only begotten Son into the world” ([1 John 4:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+4%3A9&version=NKJV)). Someone must exist as Son before he can be sent.
- **[Psalm 2:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+2%3A7&version=NKJV)**, “You are My Son, today I have begotten You,” is quoted of Christ in [Hebrews 1:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+1%3A5&version=NKJV). The Church confesses in the Creed that He is “born of the Father before all ages.”
- **[Hebrews 1:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+1%3A6&version=NKJV)** says that when the Father brings the firstborn into the world, “Let all the angels of God worship Him.” The Son is worshipped by the angels, so he cannot be one of them.

## Could the Son create the world if he only became divine later?

[John 1:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A3&version=NKJV) says, “All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made.” If the Son became divine only after his baptism or his resurrection, he could not have been the agent of creation. [John 1:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A10&version=NKJV) adds that “the world was made through Him.”

Witnesses reply that Jehovah created the Son first and then used him to create everything else. The reply runs into three difficulties:

- **[Colossians 1:16-17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+1%3A16-17&version=NKJV).** Paul writes that “by Him all things were created... all things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.” The New World Translation inserts the word “other” four times (“all other things”). Earlier editions placed it in brackets, which admits that the Greek text does not contain it. Without the insertion, Christ stands on the Creator’s side of the line, not the creature’s.
- **[Isaiah 44:24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+44%3A24&version=NKJV).** Jehovah says he stretched out the heavens “alone” and spread out the earth “by Myself.” The Son’s share in creation fits this only if he is himself Jehovah.
- **[Revelation 1:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+1%3A17&version=NKJV) and [22:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+22%3A13&version=NKJV).** Jesus calls himself “the First and the Last,” the title Jehovah claims for himself in [Isaiah 44:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+44%3A6&version=NKJV). A being who was created cannot be the first.

## “A god”? [John 1:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A1&version=NKJV) and the other titles

The New World Translation renders [John 1:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A1&version=NKJV) as “the Word was *a god*.” Greek has no indefinite article, so the choice is the translators’. The wording of the Greek, *theos ēn ho logos*, places *theos* first for emphasis and describes the **nature** of the Word. He is what the Father is.

Further problems for the “a god” reading:

- **It gives Scripture two gods.** [Isaiah 43:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+43%3A10&version=NKJV) says, “Before Me there was no God formed, nor shall there be after Me.” A “lesser god” next to Jehovah is hard to reconcile with this.
- **“Mighty God” and “Almighty God” are not separate ranks.** Witnesses read [Isaiah 9:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+9%3A6&version=NKJV) (“Mighty God”) as lower than “Almighty God.” But [Isaiah 10:21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+10%3A21&version=NKJV) uses the same Hebrew title, *El Gibbor*, of Jehovah himself, and the New World Translation also renders it “Mighty God” there.
- **[John 20:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+20%3A28&version=NKJV).** Thomas says to Jesus, “My Lord and my God!” and Jesus accepts it with a blessing, not a rebuke.
- **[John 8:58](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+8%3A58&version=NKJV) and [5:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+5%3A18&version=NKJV).** Jesus says, “Before Abraham was, I AM,” and his hearers take up stones. They understood that he was claiming equality with God. The New World Translation renders the phrase “I have been,” which obscures the link to [Exodus 3:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+3%3A14&version=NKJV).
- **Worship.** Witnesses translate *proskuneō*, when it is directed to Christ, as “obeisance.” But the same Gospels forbid worship of anyone but God ([Matthew 4:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+4%3A10&version=NKJV)), and angels and men refuse it for themselves ([Revelation 19:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+19%3A10&version=NKJV), [Acts 10:25-26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+10%3A25-26&version=NKJV)). Yet Jesus accepts it ([Matthew 28:9, 17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+28%3A9%2C+17&version=NKJV); [John 9:38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+9%3A38&version=NKJV)), and [Hebrews 1:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+1%3A6&version=NKJV) commands it.

## “The Father is greater than I”

Witnesses cite [John 14:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+14%3A28&version=NKJV), [Mark 13:32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+13%3A32&version=NKJV) and [1 Corinthians 15:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15%3A28&version=NKJV) to prove that Jesus is lesser. Historic Christianity does not deny that the Son submits to the Father. It says the submission is one of **role**, taken on in the incarnation. [Philippians 2:6-8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians+2%3A6-8&version=NKJV) describes the Son who, “being in the form of God,” did not grasp at equality but “made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant.” A servant’s obedience tells us about the mission, not about the nature of the one who is sent.

## The resurrection of the body

If the body was disposed of and Jesus rose “in spirit only,” then the resurrection did not happen as the Gospels describe it.

- **[Luke 24:39-43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+24%3A39-43&version=NKJV).** “Handle Me and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see I have.” Then he ate fish in their presence.
- **[John 20:27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+20%3A27&version=NKJV).** He invites Thomas to touch the wounds in his hands and side.
- **[John 2:19-21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+2%3A19-21&version=NKJV).** “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” John explains that “He was speaking of the temple of His body.”
- **[John 10:17-18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+10%3A17-18&version=NKJV).** Jesus says he has the authority to lay down his life and take it up again. Witnesses teach that the Father raised him as a spirit creature. The text says that he has power to take it up himself.

The meaning of resurrection is the raising of the **body**. The spirit does not die, so only the body needs raising. If the body does not rise, there is no resurrection.

Witnesses add that the wounded body would make Christ “less than the angels.” We see the wounds differently. They are the sign of redeeming love: “by His stripes we are healed” ([Isaiah 53:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+53%3A5&version=NKJV)). In heaven John sees “a Lamb as though it had been slain” ([Revelation 5:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+5%3A6&version=NKJV)), and the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders worship him because he was slain and redeemed us by his blood ([Revelation 5:8-9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+5%3A8-9&version=NKJV)).

## Immortality and the state of the dead

Witnesses say Christ gained immortality as a reward for his obedience. Because they hold that he is a creature, this is consistent with their own view, so the argument “he created everything, so he must always have been immortal” does not persuade them by itself. Better texts speak directly to the point:

- **[John 5:26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+5%3A26&version=NKJV).** “As the Father has life in Himself, so He has granted the Son to have life in Himself.”
- **[Hebrews 7:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+7%3A16&version=NKJV).** He is a priest “according to the power of an endless life.”
- **[Hebrews 13:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+13%3A8&version=NKJV).** “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever.”
- **[John 17:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+17%3A5&version=NKJV).** He asks for “the glory which I had with You before the world was.”

On the dead, **[Luke 23:43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+23%3A43&version=NKJV)** is the standard text: “Assuredly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise.” The New World Translation moves the comma: “I tell you today, You will be with me in Paradise.” The original Greek manuscripts had no punctuation, so an honest case cannot rest on the Greek alone. It rests on the rest of the New Testament. Paul says that to depart is “to be with Christ, which is far better” ([Philippians 1:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians+1%3A23&version=NKJV)), and that to be “absent from the body” is to be “present with the Lord” ([2 Corinthians 5:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Corinthians+5%3A8&version=NKJV)). Jesus also speaks of the rich man and Lazarus as conscious after death ([Luke 16:22-25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+16%3A22-25&version=NKJV)).

## Two disputed verses

Two further examples are often used, and each needs careful handling.

### [1 Timothy 3:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Timothy+3%3A16&version=NKJV)

The New King James Version reads, “God was manifested in the flesh.” The New World Translation reads, “He was made manifest in flesh.” This is a genuine question of manuscript evidence. Many modern translations, not only the New World Translation, read “He who” or “who” and not “God.” The traditional reading is defended by the great majority of later manuscripts. It is fair to present the matter as a textual debate, not as a Witness deletion. The truth of the Incarnation does not depend on this verse alone ([John 1:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A14&version=NKJV); [Colossians 2:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+2%3A9&version=NKJV); [Titus 2:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Titus+2%3A13&version=NKJV)).

### [Acts 20:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+20%3A28&version=NKJV)

The common reading is “the church of God which He purchased with His own blood.” The New World Translation reads, “with the blood of his own Son.” The Greek can be understood in more than one way, and “his own [Son’s]” is a possible reading, so it is wiser not to press the grammar. The doctrinal question is the better ground. Who purchased the Church? If the Son’s blood purchased it, and the text calls the Purchaser “God,” then the Son is God.

## Speaking with Witnesses

1. **Represent their view accurately.** Say what they believe in their own words before you answer it.
2. **Ask questions before offering answers.** “How do you understand [Colossians 1:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+1%3A16&version=NKJV)?” opens a conversation. A lecture closes one.
3. **Use their own Bible where you can.** Even the New World Translation has Thomas call Jesus “my God” ([John 20:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+20%3A28&version=NKJV)), and it records Jesus saying that he and the Father are one ([John 10:30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+10%3A30&version=NKJV)).
4. **Keep to the main point.** The question is not the vocabulary of one verse. It is who Jesus is, whom Scripture commands us to worship, and whether the One who died for us is God.

## Conclusion

The apostolic confession remains the one Peter gave:

> “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” — [Matthew 16:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+16%3A16&version=NKJV)
