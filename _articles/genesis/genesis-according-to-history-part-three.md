<h2>Introduction</h2>

<p>The following article continues to draw on Gleason L. Archer Jr., <em>A Survey of Old Testament Introduction</em> (Chicago: Moody Press), pp. 203-209. It examines four weaknesses of Darwinian evolution in the light of <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1&version=ESV">Genesis 1</a>: the evidence from genetics, the argument from embryology, adaptations with no transitional stage, and the missing links. It shows that the theory was historically developed to explain life without divine influence, which is why the author finds it a dubious course for a Christian loyal to Scripture.</p>

<p><em>A note before we begin: these are the arguments as made in the source text, which comes from the late twentieth century. Most biologists today regard evolution by natural selection as well supported, and several of the points below have been answered in the scientific literature. Read this article as a summary of one author’s case, and study both the biblical and the scientific sources for yourself.</em></p>

<p>With that said, I proceed to the summary.</p>

<h2>Main Discussion</h2>

<p>Each part below builds on the one before it, so it is best read in order.</p>

<h3>The Deeper Question</h3>

<p>More fundamental than the length of the creative days is the fact of divine creation itself. Did God create the living world, or did it arise through the mechanical process of natural selection? The answer decides whether Genesis 1 is the foundation of reality or a story that science has left behind.</p>

<h3>What Darwin Taught</h3>

<p>In <em>Origin of Species</em> (1859) Charles Darwin tried to explain the origin of species by <strong>natural selection</strong> instead of by God’s design. Each generation shows small variations from the one before.</p>

<p>Over thousands of generations some become fixed and lead to new varieties and finally new species. Those with advantages survive and those with disadvantages die off, so that the simple gradually becomes the complex until man appears as the climax.</p>

<p>On the origin of matter and of the first life, Darwin had no answer, except possibly a deistic one in which a Creator breathed life into one primordial form and then withdrew. Even so, no objective basis remained for moral law or spiritual values beyond survival. And we may ask who or what decides what counts as “fit.” This was almost a total contradiction of Genesis 1.</p>

<p>The author raises four objections.</p>

<h3>1. The Evidence from Genetics</h3>

<p>Gregor Mendel’s experiments showed that variation within a species is strictly limited. Tall peas may vary slightly in height, but the offspring of the taller ones are not on average taller than those of the shorter ones.</p>

<p>Breeders can produce many strains, such as the many types of dog, but there is a firm boundary beyond which they cannot go. No breeder can produce a new species.</p>

<p>Lamarck’s idea that acquired characteristics can be inherited fails for the same reason. Skills gained by effort do not alter the genes, and no claim for such inheritance has survived testing.</p>

<p>Mutations do occur and can produce new varieties. But after thousands of studied mutations, the author says, there is not one clear case in which a mutation made an animal more complex or brought a new structure into existence. As one reviewer wrote in <em>Nature</em>, the fundamental causes of evolution on the grand scale cannot yet be described or explained.</p>

<h3>2. The Argument from Embryology</h3>

<p>Darwin argued that the human embryo repeats its evolutionary past, and that the visceral pouches are the same organs as the gills of fish. But those structures never work as gills at any stage.</p>

<p>The theory also sits badly with the order of development. The respiratory surface appears very late in the embryo, yet no ancestor could have lived without breathing. The eye is formed from several parts developed separately and then combined according to a predetermined pattern.</p>

<p>Early resemblances between mammal embryos have a simpler explanation: simpler parts must be formed before complicated ones. Imagining that they prove common ancestry is like imagining that raindrops come from pebbles because both are round.</p>

<h4>Signs of design in the womb</h4>

<p>Embryology also shows signs of deliberate design. When one mechanism of growth fails, a completely different one sometimes takes over and produces the needed structure. Such rare “double assurance” cannot be explained by survival of the fittest.</p>

<p>Some fetuses do develop badly, with tragic results, a problem called <em>dysteleology</em>. But on Darwinian principles it is impossible to explain even the sense of pathos we feel in such cases. If nature is only a blind process, why should anything about it strike us as tragic?</p>

<h3>3. Adaptations With No Transitional Stage</h3>

<p>Natural selection requires that every stage in the development of an organ gave some advantage. But what advantage would half an eye give?</p>

<p>Even a light-sensitive patch of skin would not help an animal survive until real sight existed. There are likewise no fossil ants or termites from before their organized colony life, and it is not clear that frogs are better fitted to survive than the fish from which they are supposed to have come.</p>

<h4>The automobile analogy</h4>

<p>Structural resemblances, and even apparently useless “vestigial” parts such as the coccyx, can be explained as a general plan followed by the Designer. The author compares this to the development of the automobile from 1901 to the 1990s. Features of earlier models, like the crank opening at the base of the radiator or the “portholes” of the Buick, were kept long after they were needed.</p>

<p>Nobody concludes that cars made themselves; designers and engineers did it. Once a species was designed, it was reproduced through the built-in system of reproduction, each kept within Mendelian limits by its own set of genes.</p>

<h3>4. The Missing Links and “Emergent Evolution”</h3>

<p>Darwin expected later research to uncover transitional forms, but most twentieth-century scientists, the author says, have given up that hope. Austin H. Clark noted the complete lack of intermediates between the major groups of animals, and G. G. Simpson observed that each of the thirty-two known orders of mammals appears suddenly in the fossil record, with no continuous sequence from one order to another.</p>

<p>Their response was a theory of <em>emergent evolution</em>: dramatic new forms arise by chance or by some creative response to the environment that cannot be analyzed. The author calls this not really an explanation but an appeal for faith. As Carl Henry observes, a theory of abrupt emergence falls outside scientific analysis just as much as an appeal to supernatural creative power.</p>

<h3>Can a Christian Be an Evolutionist?</h3>

<p>Many Christians accept evolution on a theistic basis, holding that God created matter from nothing and then guided the process. The author replies that historically the whole theory was developed to explain life <strong>without</strong> divine influence. Darwin and his colleagues tried hard to overturn the argument from design and exploited every case of apparent purposelessness.</p>

<p>Evolution became the official philosophy of the materialistic movements of the twentieth century. Leading figures such as Ernst Haeckel and G. G. Simpson said openly that evolution is incompatible with belief in God’s activity in the universe.</p>

<p>For these reasons the author finds it a dubious course for a Christian loyal to Scripture to call himself an evolutionist, except in a very restricted sense that Darwin and his followers would never accept. Readers who disagree, including many thoughtful Christians, should weigh this against the case made on the other side.</p>

<h3>Why This Matters</h3>

<p>Whatever one concludes about the details, the author’s central point is worth keeping: the question is not only about fossils and genes but about whether the world has a Designer. Genesis 1 answers that every kind of plant and animal came into being by the will of an all-wise God, and that man is not an accident.</p>

<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Darwin proposed natural selection as an explanation of species without appeal to divine design.</li>
  <li>The author argues from genetics, embryology, the lack of transitional stages and the fossil record that the theory has serious weaknesses.</li>
  <li>He holds that the theory was historically built to remove God from the picture, and so finds it dubious for a Christian to call himself an evolutionist.</li>
  <li>These are the arguments of a late twentieth-century source, and readers should test them against current scientific literature.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>The affirmation of <a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1&version=ESV">Genesis 1</a>, that every kind of plant and animal came into being by the will of an all-wise God, explains the data of biology far better, in the author’s view, than mechanical natural selection. In <a href="{{ gp4.url | relative_url }}">PT 4</a>, the final part, we turn to the antiquity of man, the genealogies of Genesis, and the question of Adam.</p>
